In modern autonomous systems, efficient coverage is essential for surveillance, data collection, and environmental monitoring. Adaptive coverage control enables mobile agents to strategically position themselves based on real-time information, with applications in crowd monitoring, precision agriculture, and environmental sensing.

Autonomous systems, particularly UAVs, often operate under constraints such as limited flight time, sensing capabilities, and communication resources. Distributed coverage algorithms enable agents to coordinate their movements and efficiently cover designated regions of the environment.

Using Voronoi-based partitioning, each agent is responsible for a specific region and communicates primarily with its Voronoi neighbours. This decentralized approach reduces communication requirements and improves scalability in multi-agent systems.

Objectives of Distributed Adaptive Coverage Control

The primary objectives of Distributed Adaptive Coverage Control (DACC) are:

  1. Coverage Optimization: Drive mobile agents toward optimal positions by minimizing a locational cost function associated with the coverage task.
  2. Sensory Function Estimation: Estimate the unknown spatial distribution of the quantity of interest (sensory function) using measurements collected by the agents.
